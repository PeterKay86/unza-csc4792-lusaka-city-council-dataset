promote inclusive urban growth and improved quality of life for all residents. Guided by the Eighth
National Development Plan (8NDP), the Integrated Development Plan (IDP), and the Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs), the Council will strengthen systems for transparent governance, resource
mobilization, and accountable service delivery. The Council will prioritize infrastructure development,
environmental resilience, and social inclusion through evidence-based planning, community
participation, and innovation. By fostering strategic partnerships with stakeholders, enhancing digital
transformation, and mainstreaming climate action, Lusaka City Council will ensure a well-planned,
healthy, safe, and economically vibrant city for present and future generations.

LUSAKA CITY COUNCIL
The Lusaka City Council’s approved budget for 2026 stands at K1.2 billion, representing a 22.5% increase
from the 2025 allocation of K939.9 million. This growth is driven by strategic investments in community
development through the expanded Constituency Development Fund (CDF), which continues to finance
critical community projects, bursaries, and empowerment initiatives across the city’s seven
constituencies, alongside the implementation of a new Valuation Roll that strengthens the Council’s
revenue base through improved property rate assessments. The 2026 budget is organized around sixteen
strategic programmes, designed to advance the priorities of the Eighth National Development Plan
(8NDP), support Lusaka’s Integrated Development Plan (IDP) objectives, and contribute to the
achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Key focus areas include economic
transformation, inclusive human and social development, environmental sustainability, good
governance, and resilient urban infrastructure. By aligning local investments with national and global
development frameworks, resources are directed to interventions that generate measurable impact,
enhance service delivery, promote social equity, and foster sustainable growth. Overall, the 2026 budget
underscores Lusaka City Council’s commitment to inclusive development, community empowerment,
fiscal responsibility, and long-term strategic planning, while maintaining strong alignment with local,
national, and global development agendas.

LUSAKA CITY COUNCIL
In 2026, Lusaka City Council has strategically allocated resources across its programmes and
subprogrammes to strengthen service delivery, promote community empowerment, and support
sustainable development. The Constituency Development Fund CDF receives K280.2 million, with K170.5
million dedicated to community projects and infrastructure, K48.5 million earmarked for women and
youth empowerment, K48.5 million for secondary school bursaries and skills development, and K12.8
million for programme administration. These allocations reflect a strong commitment to participatory
development across Lusaka’s seven constituencies. The Local Governance Programme is allocated K28.8
million, supporting legislative functions, citizen engagement, and Ward Development Fund initiatives,
including K1.4 million for Ward Development Committees, K9.4 million to strengthen legislative
operations, and K18.0 million to enhance citizen participation in addressing priority community needs
such as roads, water, and sanitation. The Integrated Development Planning Programme receives K33.8
million, with K24.3 million directed to spatial planning, K7.8 million to socio-economic planning, and K1.7
million for environmental planning, ensuring that Lusaka’s growth is coordinated, evidence-based, and
resilient. In support of economic transformation, the Economic and Business Development Programme
is allocated K49.9 million, including K15.7 million for trade facilitation and licensing, and K34.2 million for
Local Economic Development, which encompasses the completion and equipping of three mini civic
centres to promote entrepreneurship and local business growth. Health and well-being are prioritized
with the Public Health and Environmental Protection Programme receiving K73.6 million, mainly for
health inspections K71.1 million and pest control K2.4 million, safeguarding public health standards
across the city. The Housing and Community Amenities Programme is allocated K146.9 million,
distributed across roads and drainage K28.2 million, street lighting K31.0 million, parks and gardens K6.8
million, markets and bus stations K23.3 million, community centres K50.1 million, and public housing
K7.5 million, reflecting Lusaka’s commitment to quality infrastructure and safe, vibrant
communities. Cultural and recreational development receives attention through the Recreation, Culture,
and Religion Programme with K12.2 million, covering sports promotion K8.2 million, library services K3.9
million, and cultural affairs K1,724, fostering learning, creative expression, and social cohesion.

million, including K23.0 million for early childhood education, K10.8 million for adult literacy, and

In 2026, Lusaka City Council will implement the Constituency Development Programme (CDF) with a focus
on inclusive service delivery, infrastructure development, and community empowerment across all seven
constituencies. Under infrastructure development, the programme will prioritise the construction and paving
of over 15 kilometres of township roads to improve access to health facilities, schools, and markets. Major
road works will be undertaken in Chawama Constituency, including Anglican Road in Chawama Ward 2 and
Ngwenya Road in Nkholoma Ward 1, among others. In Kabwata Constituency, paving will focus on key
township roads in Libala, Kamwala, Kamulanga, and Kabwata Wards, while Kanyama Constituency will see
significant investments in access roads serving John Laing Clinic, Kanyama West Clinic, and surrounding
communities in Chinika, Makeni Villa, and Garden Park Wards. Selected township roads will also be
improved in Mandevu and Lusaka Central, including market access roads in Silwizya Ward. Complementing
road works, drainage infrastructure will be constructed in flood-prone areas of John Howard Ward
(Chawama) and Mulungushi Ward (Mandevu) to enhance climate resilience and protect road
investments. Social infrastructure investments will include the construction of classroom blocks at Bayuni
School in Munkolo Ward (Kanyama) and Muchinga School in Mwembeshi Ward (Matero), the construction
of an Out-Patient Department at Kuku Health Post in Nkholoma Ward (Chawama), and maternity wings in
Ngwerere (Mandevu) and Kamwala (Kabwata). Community safety will be strengthened through the
construction of police posts in Silwizya (Lusaka Central) and Kapwepwe (Matero), and a new police station in
Muchinga (Matero). Through rehabilitation works, the programme will upgrade selected township roads in
Chinika and Makeni Villa Wards, rehabilitate schools, clinics, and police posts in Independence and John
Howard Wards, and improve sports and recreation facilities in Lubwa, Lima, and Matero, among others. To
enhance service delivery capacity, the programme will finance the procurement of four Tractor Loader
Backhoes (TLBs), the distribution of over 2,100 school desks, the equipping of clinics with medical and
maternity equipment, and the installation of solar streetlights and water supply systems in priority wards,
including Munkolo, Kabwata, Roma, and Kabanana, among others. In parallel, the programme will provide
funding support to 800 loan beneficiaries, 600 grant recipients, and 800 women and youth cooperatives. To
strengthen governance and accountability, 76 community communication campaigns will be conducted, 119
CDFC members trained, and 38 Ward Development Committees supported, with all  projects monitored in
line with national CDF guidelines. Additionally, 7,000 skills development bursaries and 1,500 secondary
school bursaries will be awarded to expand access to education and economic opportunity across Lusaka.

Executive Authority:
Programmes under which this Council will be accounted for by the Town Clerk, Lusaka City Council
In 2026, Lusaka City Council will implement the Social Protection and Community Development Programme
to provide comprehensive support to vulnerable populations, strengthening social safety nets and
promoting inclusive economic participation. Through the Cash for Work Programme, 15,379 beneficiaries
will gain temporary employment and income support, with K59.6 million disbursed to facilitate productive
engagement, boost household incomes, and empower communities to meet immediate needs. Beyond cash
transfers, the programme will deliver targeted welfare services to 25 households, providing essential
support to those most in need, including the elderly, persons with disabilities, and marginalized groups.
These combined interventions will not only alleviate short-term economic hardship but also enhance social
cohesion, improve community resilience, and lay the groundwork for sustainable livelihoods, positioning
Lusaka City Council as a catalyst for equitable social development across the district.
